The story behind every stitch

A Craft Older Than the City's Bridges

From the courts of Awadh to your wardrobe

Chikankari began in the royal workshops of 18th-century Lucknow — delicate white-thread embroidery on fine muslin, said to have been favoured by the Mughal court. Two hundred years on, the same stitches — bakhiya, hool, phanda — are still worked entirely by hand, needle by needle.

THE CHICKANKARI STUDIO was started with one simple aim: to bring this unhurried, handmade craft to people who value it, without diluting what makes it real.

How Each Piece is Made

Six Stages, One Needle

Design

Motifs are hand-block-printed onto fabric — florals, paisleys, jaali patterns rooted in Awadhi tradition.

Embroidery

Karigars hand-stitch each motif, often taking several days per garment depending on density of work.

Washing

Pieces are washed to remove printed guide-lines, revealing the pure thread work underneath.

Finishing

Edges, buttons and linings are finished by hand before a final quality check.

Pressing

Each garment is carefully pressed to let the embroidery sit right, without flattening the texture.

Packing

Wrapped and boxed with care, ready to travel from Lucknow to your door.
